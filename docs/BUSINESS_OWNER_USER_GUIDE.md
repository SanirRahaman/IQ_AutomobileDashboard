# How to use yoyotaDealers

## A simple guide for a dealership owner or sales manager

You do not need to understand code to use this website. It helps you answer:

- How is the business performing?
- Are we reaching our sales targets?
- Which branch or salesperson needs attention?
- Which customers should the team follow up with?
- Where are sales or deliveries slowing down?

Use it to prepare questions and follow-up work for your team. The website shows the
records supplied to it; it does not update customer records or contact customers.

## 1. Start here: your first five minutes

1. **Check “Data as of.”** This tells you how recent the information is.
2. **Choose the period** you want to review, such as one month.
3. **Leave “All branches” selected** to see the overall business first.
4. **Read the six summary cards** near the top and the context directly below each value.
5. **Read “What needs attention.”** Compare its metric with the shown benchmark, then open the exact supporting records.
6. **Check deliveries vs target and operational health** for target gaps, stale value, and delivery timing.
7. **Use the management gates** to see whether losses concentrate before contact, test drive, or close; open the full journey only when you need stage detail.
8. **Choose a branch and then a representative** if you need to understand who should follow up.
9. **Download a follow-up list** when you want to take the open opportunities into a team discussion.

You can return to the starting view with **Reset**.

## 2. Check the date before making a decision

There are two different dates to understand.

**The selected period** is the period you are asking about. For example, selecting
December asks the dashboard to show December's results.

**“Data as of”** is the latest recorded business date in the supplied information.
The current supplied dataset shows **31 Dec 2025**. This date comes from the data;
it is not the date on your computer.

If you open this dataset much later, it still describes that historical snapshot.
“Overdue” and “Needs follow-up” are measured at that snapshot. Before contacting a
customer, ask your team whether there has been more recent activity outside this dataset.

Changing the selected period does not change the snapshot date.

## 3. Choose what you want to see

### Review a month or a date range

Use **Month** to choose an available calendar month. Use the date-range control if
you need a different start and end date.

The selected period is shown near the top. Check it again before sharing figures
with your team, especially after moving between pages.

### Review one branch

Open **All branches** and choose a branch. The cards and findings update for that
branch. Use **Review branch & representatives** to open its detailed view.

### Review one representative

After choosing a branch, use the representative control where available, or open
the branch's detail page and select a name under **Sales representatives**.

Use **Back to branch** to move back up. **All branches** or **Overview** takes you
toward the overall view. Returning to the overview can retain your selected period;
use **Reset** when you want to clear all filters.

### Narrow the view further

Use **More filters** on the dashboard, or **Filters** on the operational pages,
to narrow results by options such as lead source, vehicle model or lead status.

Filters work together. The complete active scope is written below the selected period,
including secondary filters. For example, a branch plus a vehicle model shows only matching
records for that branch and model. A very narrow selection may produce no records.
That does not necessarily mean there is a problem with the website.

### Save a view for later

The web address changes with your filters. Bookmark it or copy it to reopen the same
view against the loaded dataset. Refresh and your browser's Back/Forward buttons
restore the corresponding filters.

A saved link saves the view settings, not a permanent copy of the figures. If the
underlying dataset is replaced later, the results may change.

## 4. Understand the summary cards

### Leads received

The number of potential sales first received during the selected period and filters.
This tells you how much new sales interest came in.

### Vehicles delivered

The number of recorded vehicle deliveries during the selected period. Some may have
started as leads in an earlier month.

**Example:** A customer first enquires in November and receives a vehicle in December.
That customer belongs to November's leads received and December's deliveries.
This is why the delivery card may differ from the delivered count in a lead-outcome view.

### Resolved conversion

Of the leads in this view that have reached a final outcome, how many were delivered?

For example, if 10 were delivered, 10 were lost and 5 are still active, resolved
conversion is **50%**. The 5 active opportunities are kept separate because the team
is still working on them.

This percentage alone does not tell the full story. Read it alongside the number of
leads, active opportunities and how long those opportunities have been open.
A dash means there is not enough applicable information to calculate the figure;
it does not mean zero performance.

### Active opportunity value

The summed deal value on potential sales that have not been delivered or lost. The
number below it gives the count of active opportunities. These might be new enquiries,
contacted customers, test drives, negotiations or placed orders awaiting completion.

An active opportunity is **not automatically a lost sale**.

### Target attainment

How much of the applicable supplied vehicle-delivery target has been achieved.
For example, 40 actual deliveries against a target of 50 means **80% attained** and
**10 below target**. These are example numbers, not fixed figures in the website.

### Needs follow-up

The count of active opportunities identified as stale or overdue at the order stage,
as of the dataset date. Start here when planning a follow-up discussion with the team.
It is a review list, not proof that a representative has done something wrong.

**Tip:** Select a card's information icon for its specific definition.

## 5. Read “Deliveries vs target”

For each available comparison, read:

- **Actual:** recorded deliveries included in the comparison.
- **Target:** the supplied target for the matching branch and month or months.
- **Attainment:** the percentage achieved.
- **Gap:** how many vehicles above or below the target.

A useful next question is: “Which branch has a gap, and what do its active
opportunities and sales stages tell us to investigate?”

Keep these limits in mind:

- Targets are supplied by branch and month. The website does not invent personal targets.
- Some filters, such as a representative or model, make a branch-wide target unsuitable.
  In that case, the comparison is not shown.
- A partial month is labelled. Its full monthly target has not been reduced to an
  assumed daily target. A remaining gap is not a final month-end result.
- Missing or unusable targets can limit coverage. Read the panel's explanation.
- The supplied records may not cover every sale behind the target. Confirm coverage
  with your team before treating the displayed gap as the complete business result.

When a suitable complete month is selected, you may also see deliveries compared
with the previous month. This shows the change in recorded output, not the cause.

## 6. Use “What needs attention”

Each finding shows its observed metric, benchmark when applicable, affected records
and value, business meaning, suggested next step, and supporting records.
A practical way to use it is:

1. Read what was observed.
2. Check the selected branch and period.
3. Read the suggested action after **Next:**.
4. Open **View … supporting records**.
5. Check a few individual records before deciding what the team should do.

Priority labels help you decide what to inspect first. They do not prove the cause
of a problem. A weak conversion figure, for example, does not by itself prove poor
sales effort; customer mix, timing or incomplete records may also matter.

Open the additional findings if you want to investigate beyond the first few shown.

## 7. Go from branch performance to a team conversation

Suppose you want to investigate a branch that is below its delivery target.

1. Choose that branch from the branch filter.
2. Read its delivered vehicles, active opportunities and resolved conversion.
3. Open **Review branch & representatives**.
4. Compare its results with the displayed wider-business figures.
5. Find **Sales representatives** and select a representative's name.
6. Review their workload, outcomes and sales-stage progress.
7. Open **View / export active opportunities** if you need the underlying open records.

Useful questions to ask the team include:

- Which customers are waiting for the next contact?
- Which orders need a confirmed expected date?
- Are records up to date?
- Is the difficulty concentrated at one stage of the sales process?
- What specific next step has been agreed for each customer?

Compare like with like. A representative with only a few leads is not directly
comparable with one handling a much larger workload. Use the dashboard to prepare
a discussion, not as the only basis for judging an employee.

## 8. Review active opportunities

Open **Active opportunities** to understand unfinished sales.

- **Pipeline by stage** shows where open opportunities currently sit.
- **Inactivity distribution** shows how long it has been since recorded activity.
- **Opportunity age distribution** shows how long opportunities have existed.
- **Expected-close and order backlog** highlights open work around expected dates.
- **Oldest active opportunities** helps you find individual records to review.
- Branch and representative breakdowns help you find the relevant team.

Age and inactivity are different. A long-running sale may have recent activity,
while a newer lead may already have been left without follow-up.

Labels such as healthy, watch, stale and critical describe attention bands based on
recorded inactivity. They do not change the customer's actual status to lost.

The period filter still matters here: the active-opportunity view concerns leads
received in the selected period. To review the broadest available open list, clear
the date filter rather than assuming one selected month contains every active lead.

## 9. Review delivery performance

Open **Delivery performance** to see how long recorded deliveries took and where
delays are associated with branches, vehicles or recorded reasons.

Use it to ask which delivery processes deserve a closer look. A reason being linked
to longer delivery time is not proof that it caused the entire delay.

Read the explanation around **Expected-close timeliness** carefully. The supplied
expected-close date is not confirmed to mean a promised physical delivery date, so
this comparison is an indicator to investigate, not a confirmed promise-breach count.

## 10. Inspect a customer record and download a follow-up list

### Inspect a record

Open a supporting-record list, then select a customer. The detail view can show
contact information, vehicle, current status, assigned branch/representative,
expected-close date, last activity and recorded status history.

Use the history to understand what has already happened. A listed value has no
assumed currency unless the source data establishes one.

### Download a list

1. Choose the correct branch, period and other filters first.
2. Open a finding's supporting records or **View / export active opportunities**.
3. Choose **Download follow-up** for active opportunities only.
4. Choose **All … records · CSV** if you need every record behind that list,
   including resolved outcomes where present.
5. Open the downloaded CSV in Excel, Google Sheets or another spreadsheet program.

The file includes useful fields such as customer, phone, branch, representative,
vehicle, status, important dates and reason for review. Its filename describes the
view. If there are no active records, an active follow-up download may not be offered.

The file respects the selected list and filters. A finding's download is its
supporting list, not necessarily every active opportunity in the branch.

Ask the relevant manager to agree next actions using your normal working process.
Downloading does not send the file to staff, contact customers or mark work complete
inside this website. Handle customer contact details through your normal business
privacy and sharing practices.

## 11. Understand warnings and recent-month results

### Data-quality notices

These flag records that need checking, such as a current status that disagrees with
the last recorded history entry. Open the notices before relying heavily on affected
information. The website does not silently rewrite those records.

Corrections must happen in the source-data process; this dashboard has no record editor.

### “Immature” in conversion by lead month

This means a group of newer leads has not had enough observation time for a reliable
final-performance judgement. It does not mean the customers are lost or the branch
is definitely underperforming.

You can still inspect the figures, but avoid comparing them as though they had the
same time to finish as older lead groups.

### A dash or no records

A dash normally means a figure is unavailable or cannot be calculated sensibly.
“No records match these filters” means the current combination has no matching data.
Try clearing one filter or selecting **Reset**.

## 12. A simple routine for an owner

### For a quick review

Check the data date and period, read the summary cards, look at targets, and choose
one or two findings to discuss with the team.

### For a branch review

Choose the branch, check its target context, inspect representative workload and
open the relevant active records. Agree specific next steps outside the dashboard.

### For a monthly review

Choose a complete month. Review delivery output and the available previous-month
comparison. Check target coverage and distinguish delivery results from newly
received lead outcomes. Treat immature lead groups cautiously.

If the dataset has not been refreshed, another review shows the same historical
information. The website is not a live feed of today's dealership activity.

## 13. If something looks wrong

- **The figures changed unexpectedly:** check the period, branch, representative
  and More filters. Use Reset to return to the broad view.
- **A target disappeared:** check whether a representative, source, model or status
  filter is selected, or whether target coverage is missing.
- **Deliveries do not match delivered lead outcomes:** one uses delivery dates and
  the other follows leads received in the selected period; see section 4.
- **The information seems old:** check Data as of. Ask whoever maintains the dataset
  whether a newer extract is available.
- **A record seems incorrect:** inspect its history and quality notices, then report
  the record reference to the person responsible for the source information.
- **The dataset could not be loaded:** use Try again. If it continues, report the
  displayed message to the person maintaining the website.

You do not need the developer handbook for normal use. The main habit is simple:
check the date and filters, inspect the evidence, and agree a clear next action with
the team.

## 14. Answer a specific question with Explore performance

Open **Explore performance** below the overview cards. Your branch, representative,
source, model, status and date filters stay applied. Use **Filters** on this page
to change them. Data filters stay in the URL; the selected measure, tab and sort
order are temporary presentation choices and reset after a refresh.

### “Which car sold the most? Which sold the least?”

1. Choose **Comparisons → Vehicle models → Delivered vehicles**.
2. Choose **Highest first** or **Lowest first**. Choose **All results** to see every model.
3. Click a model's bar to inspect its delivery records.

Sales counts use delivery dates. A model with zero deliveries has no delivery list.
To ask about value instead, select **Delivered deal value**. Value is not profit.

### “Which branch or salesperson performed best for a particular vehicle?”

1. Open **Filters** and choose the vehicle model and period.
2. Choose **Branches** or **Representatives** in Comparisons.
3. Choose the measure you mean: deliveries, delivered value, or resolved conversion.
4. For rates, read the eligible/resolved lead count and any immature-cohort warning.

You can use the same steps with a lead-source filter. Clear a branch filter to
compare representatives across the network. There is no unexplained “best overall” score.

### “Compare two branches, models, sources or representatives.”

1. Choose the group and measure.
2. Enable **Compare two**, then select **A** and **B**.
3. Switch measures to check demand, value, conversion, inactivity or delivery speed.
4. Open the supporting records for either group.

Both groups use the same filters and period. Higher is not always better; fewer
delivery days or less stale value may be preferable.

### “Are sales, enquiries or conversion changing over time?”

1. Choose **Monthly trends**.
2. Select delivered vehicles, lead demand, delivered deal value, resolved conversion,
   or median delivery days.
3. Hover or select a point, or use **Selected month** to see its exact figure.
4. Read the previous-month change and open **View month records**.

Hollow points warn about partial periods or immature conversion cohorts. A gap
means the measure is unavailable. Leads/conversion use the month leads arrived;
deliveries use the month delivery occurred. Select a range covering both months to
see their change. Changes are withheld when the periods are not comparable.

### “Which opportunities are most valuable, inactive or overdue?”

1. Choose **Follow-up lists** and open active, stale or overdue opportunities.
2. In the dialog choose **Highest value first**, **Longest inactive (active)** or
   **Most overdue (active)**.
3. Select a customer to read the timeline, then download a follow-up CSV.

Downloads keep the chosen order. “Oldest lead” means time since the lead arrived;
“longest inactive” means time since its last recorded activity. Those are different.
Delivery lists also offer longest/shortest delivery sorting.

### “What was our stale pipeline last month? How much profit did we make?”

These are not supported by this view. A past date filter does not rewind the
pipeline: active status and inactivity still use **Data as of**. Profit, customer
satisfaction, marketing ROI and predictions need additional data or methods.
Target reporting currently covers supplied branch-month delivery-unit targets;
it does not evaluate revenue targets. Read **What this data can and cannot tell you**
on the exploration page when unsure.
